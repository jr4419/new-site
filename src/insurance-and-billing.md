## Verify your coverage before you come

Whatever is listed here, your own plan is the final word. Before your appointment, call the member services number on the back of your insurance card and ask:

- Is **Thomas F. Qin, MD** (Grand Central Vascular Surgery) in network under my plan?
- Do I need a **referral** from my primary care physician to see a vascular surgeon?
- What is my **deductible**, and how much of it have I met this year?
- What is my **copay or coinsurance** for a specialist visit, and for a diagnostic ultrasound performed in the office?

It is worth writing down the date, the representative's name, and the reference number for the call.

## Referrals and prior authorization

**Referrals.** Some plans — HMOs in particular — require a referral from your primary care physician before a specialist visit will be covered. If your plan requires one, please arrange it before your appointment; without it, the visit may be your responsibility.

**Prior authorization.** Procedures generally require approval from your insurer in advance. The office submits the request along with the clinical documentation, and approval typically takes several business days to a few weeks depending on the plan. We will let you know when authorization comes through, and we do not schedule an elective procedure before it does.

## What insurance usually covers

**Medically necessary care is generally covered.** That includes evaluation and treatment of symptomatic varicose veins, chronic venous insufficiency and leg ulcers, deep vein thrombosis, peripheral artery disease, carotid disease, aneurysm surveillance, and dialysis access — along with the diagnostic ultrasound used to evaluate them.

**Vein procedures usually come with conditions.** Most plans will approve treatment of varicose veins only after there is documentation of symptoms such as pain, heaviness, swelling, bleeding, or skin changes, an ultrasound demonstrating reflux, and a trial of conservative therapy — typically compression stockings worn consistently for a set number of weeks. The exact requirements differ by plan, and we will tell you what yours asks for.

**Cosmetic treatment is not covered.** Treatment of spider veins and other purely cosmetic concerns is considered elective by essentially every insurer and is offered on a self-pay basis. If a cosmetic concern turns out to have an underlying medical cause, the medical portion of the care may be covered even though the cosmetic portion is not.

## Self-pay and good faith estimates

If you are uninsured, or insured but choosing not to use your coverage for a service, you are entitled to a **good faith estimate** of the expected charges in writing before the service — a right under the federal No Surprises Act. Ask the office for one at any time; you do not need to have an appointment scheduled to request it.

Fees for cosmetic and other self-pay services are quoted in advance, in writing, before anything is scheduled.

## Questions about a bill

Call the office and ask for billing. It is worth calling us before paying a bill you do not understand or that looks wrong — many billing questions turn out to be a coding or coordination-of-benefits issue that we can resolve directly with your insurer.
