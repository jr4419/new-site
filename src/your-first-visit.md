## What to bring

- **Photo ID and your insurance card.** If you are covered under someone else's plan, bring that card and the policyholder's date of birth.
- **A referral,** if your plan requires one. See [Insurance and Billing](/insurance-and-billing.html) for how to check.
- **A list of your medications,** including doses — prescriptions, over-the-counter medicines, and supplements. A photo of the bottles works just as well as a written list.
- **Prior imaging.** Reports and, if you have them, discs or files from any previous ultrasound, CT, MRI, or angiogram. Prior studies let us compare rather than start over.
- **Your physicians' names and phone numbers** — primary care and any specialists involved in your care.
- **Your compression stockings,** if you already wear them, so we can check the fit and the strength.

## How to prepare

- **Wear something loose.** For a leg evaluation, the legs need to be examined and scanned from the groin to the ankle. You will change into shorts or a gown, so clothing that comes off easily helps.
- **Skip lotion on your legs** the morning of your appointment. Lotion interferes with the contact between the ultrasound probe and the skin.
- **If your visit is for an abdominal study** — the aorta, or the mesenteric or renal arteries — you will usually be asked not to eat for several hours beforehand, and to avoid gum and carbonated drinks, because gas in the bowel obscures the picture. Take your usual medications with a sip of water unless you are told otherwise. The office will confirm this when your appointment is scheduled.
- **Do not stop any medication on your own,** including blood thinners and aspirin. Nothing at a first visit requires you to be off them, and stopping without instruction can be dangerous.
- **Leg vein studies require no fasting or preparation,** and are performed while you stand so the veins fill normally.

## What happens during the visit

1. **Your history.** Dr. Qin will go through your symptoms, how long they have been present, what makes them better or worse, and your medical and family history.
2. **Examination.** Pulses, skin, temperature, swelling, and — for vein problems — an examination performed standing, which is when abnormal veins are most visible.
3. **Vascular ultrasound.** Most evaluations include a duplex ultrasound performed in the office. It is painless and uses no radiation, dye, or needles. Warm gel is applied and a probe is moved over the skin. Depending on what is being studied, it takes roughly twenty to forty minutes.
4. **Review of the findings.** Dr. Qin goes over the images with you and explains what they show in plain language.
5. **A plan.** You will leave with a diagnosis or a clear next step: further testing, medical management, conservative treatment, or a procedure.

## After the visit

- **Treatment is almost always scheduled for a separate day.** The first visit is for diagnosis, so you can go home, think it over, and ask questions before committing to anything.
- **For vein treatment, the clock often starts at this visit.** Most insurers require documented symptoms and a trial of conservative therapy — properly fitted compression stockings, elevation, and exercise — over a period of weeks before they will approve ablation or other procedures. Starting that documented trial early keeps things moving.
- **Records go to your other physicians.** With your permission, we send a summary of the visit and the ultrasound findings to your referring physician and primary care doctor.
